What makes it Quest Forge

Built, not assembled

Four things no other pack has. All of it in the open, on GitHub.

A hand-built questline

Twenty chapters. The numbered ones are the climb, and each opens when a boss dies: Witchery at the Naga, the End at the Hydra, TragicMC at Mobzilla. The rest are how you stay alive on the way up.

Enchantments you gamble for

None of them come from a table. Break the seal on a tome, drag the book onto your gear, and it rolls its own odds. A bad roll can shatter the item. Scrolls tilt the odds.

Worlds to reach

The Twilight Forest, Witchery's three realms, OreSpawn's six, a dozen from the Legends packs, and the inside of a Compact Machine. Every one has its own painted loading screen.

Voice chat, built in

The pack's own mod carries proximity voice: hold a key and anyone within forty blocks hears you, loudest up close. The same mod runs a jukebox that plays your own music, and lottery tickets that unlock commands.

Light and air

The pack's own shaders

Written from scratch for the pack's client. Near-photoreal light on unmistakably Minecraft geometry: realism from the sky, the sun, water and weather, never from smoothing away the blocks.

Tier A

Readable darkness

Nights and caves are dark, never black. The floor scales with the moon, and a held torch works.

Tier A

Calm exposure, neutral colour

No pumping when you look at the sky or a lamp, and no orange wash at golden hour. Wood, gold and sand are the test surfaces.

Tier C

Modded blocks are first class

Modded leaves wave, lamps glow, ores glint, and every one of the pack's dimensions gets a sky and fog of its own.
