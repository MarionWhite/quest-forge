Where the ore is

Each bar spans the height band an ore generates between — the same numbers a miner would use to pick a Y level. Bar thickness is veins per chunk, so a thick short bar is a dense seam in a narrow band, and a thin long one is scattered through the depth of the world.

How it was measured

Nothing can be asked

No registry reports how common an ore is. The ore and its vein size are fields on a generator object, but the frequency and height range are a loop bound and two arguments at the call site — there is no value to read.

So watch it happen

Every vein in the game is placed by one call to WorldGenMinable.generate. Hooking it reports the ore, the vein size and the position once per vein. Counting calls per chunk gives the frequency exactly, for vanilla and mods alike.

Ore needs neighbours

Ore is placed during populate, which refuses to run until a chunk's neighbours exist. Isolated chunks come out as bare stone. So each sample generates a 24×24 patch and counts only the middle 21×21.

Removing a mod is not subtraction

Hbm's Nuclear Tech was pulled from the pack, taking 24 of the 71 ore blocks with it. Deleting its rows looked sufficient — an ore's veins per chunk is a loop bound in its own mod, which nothing else can reach. It was not. Railcraft's poor ores rose up to sixtyfold once hbm stopped consuming the stone they need, so the whole pack was measured again rather than edited.

Runs are summed, never mixed

Three servers on three seeds measured every dimension, and their counts add: total veins over total chunks. That is only valid because all three ran the same mods. Adding a run that could not have contained an ore divides its veins by chunks that never held any — the failure that once made every rate here read thirteen times low.

Measured twice

Ore blocks were also counted directly in finished chunks. Two unrelated methods over identical chunks caught three errors that neither would have shown alone — each looked entirely plausible on its own.
