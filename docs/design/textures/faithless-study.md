# Faithless: what it does well, element by element (2026-10-25)

The owner likes "a lot of things" from **Faithless**. This study lists what the pack does, so he
can confirm the parts he likes one by one in the Shader Lab (round 7 or 8, scenes `fl-*`).

## What our stack can do (the feasibility key)

**The stack:** 1.7.10, Forge 10.13.4.1614, Angelica 2.2.x with its bundled MCPatcherForge, and
our deferred shader.

**Plain resource-pack textures: yes.**
- Block, item, entity, GUI, particle, font, painting and logo textures can all be replaced.
- So can `.mcmeta` animations and `sounds.json`.

**MCPatcherForge modules.** Angelica ships them all; only CTM is on by default. Checked in
`C:\qfb\client-ang2\config\mcpatcherforge.cfg`.

| Module | State | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `connected_textures` | **on** | CTM, random and repeat textures, and per-metadata `fixed` tiles. It has no `overlay` method. |
| `custom_item_textures` | off, one switch | MCPatcher CIT: per-damage, per-NBT and per-enchantment item textures. |
| `random_mobs` | off, one switch | Random entity texture variants. |
| `custom_colors` | off | Biome and colour maps. |
| `better_skies` | off | Skyboxes. Our shader draws the sky, so this does not apply. |
| `extended_hd` (HDFont) | off | HD fonts. |

- These modules are labelled alpha. Each switch needs a trial (`trial.py`) before it goes live.

**Not possible with a resource pack:**
- **Block models.** 1.7.10 has no JSON block models, so block shapes come from code.
  - That rules out 3D lanterns, braced fences, 3D crafting tables and any other "3D model" a
    1.20 pack does.
  - A shape change needs a renderer in our own mod (qf-content or qf-tweaks). That is code work,
    block by block.
- **Entity models.** No CEM: mob geometry is fixed, only the skins change.
- **Emissive textures.** OptiFine's emissive textures do not exist here, but our shader can do the
  same job: LabPBR `_s` alpha is emission, and the material IDs already make blocks glow.
- **Subtitles.** 1.7.10 has none. Colour-coded subtitles would need a mod.
- **GUI layout.** A container GUI can be repainted inside its own frame. Extra panels outside the
  frame, such as a recipe chart beside it or tooltip lines, need code (a qf-content GUI overlay
  or an existing mod).
- **Blocks that 1.7.10 lacks.** Deepslate, copper, lanterns, chains, beehives, coloured beds,
  observers, crimson and warped wood, and the like. Those elements only transfer as an idea.

## The checklist (26 elements)

Each entry gives:
- **Does:** what Faithless does.
- **Praised by:** who praises it.
- **Shot:** an official screenshot.
- **Feasible:** whether our stack can do it.
- **Our take:** how we'd draw our own.

### Art style

**1. Programmer-art nostalgia over vanilla palettes**
- **Does:** Every texture is redrawn, but each block keeps its vanilla colour family: oak stays
  warm brown, stone stays grey. The look recalls 2010–2013 Minecraft art, the "Programmer Art"
  era and Fallen Kingdom-style videos: chunky shapes, few tones, hand-placed pixels. There is
  little of the dithered noise in modern vanilla.
- **Praised by:** the author's stated aim (Modrinth, CurseForge). anvilpacks calls it a "nostalgic-yet-fresh"
  full overhaul with "a distinct RPG-inspired personality".
- **Shot:** Spritesheet, Thumbnail.
- **Feasible:** yes, textures only.
- **Our take:** the owner wants Minecraft's own designs with detail added. The idea we can use is
  hand-placed clusters and fewer tones per block on top of Faithful 32x shapes, not a new style.

**2. Dark, consistent outlines on items and icons**
- **Does:** Every inventory icon has a dark 1-pixel outline with a lit top-left and a shaded
  bottom-right. Icons read crisply on any slot colour.
- **Praised by:** the author (textures "designed to be clear and easy to tell apart", CurseForge).
- **Shot:** Creative tabs, Crafting.
- **Feasible:** yes (item textures).
- **Our take:** an outline-and-shading pass in our item build script, applied to vanilla and
  modded items alike. That also gives the pack's hundreds of modded items one look.

**3. Calm, low-noise terrain**
- **Does:** Grass tops, dirt, snow and sand use broad, soft clusters with little pixel noise. In
  the gallery, large surfaces read as one material, not a speckled carpet.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery. It matches the owner's own note on
  Faithful's "noisy green carpet".
- **Shot:** Farm animals (grass), Snowy village (snow, dirt), Main Menu (sand).
- **Feasible:** yes.
- **Our take:** QF v0 already smooths vanilla grass toward a blur. Next we would go further:
  broader clusters on dirt, sand, snow and gravel too.

**4. High readability through contrast between neighbours**
- **Does:** Neighbouring blocks differ clearly in value and pattern: bricks against stone bricks,
  log ends against planks. No two building blocks look alike in the creative grid.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks ("shape-based identification and high-contrast designs help even
  players with good vision").
- **Shot:** Creative tabs ("Building Blocks").
- **Feasible:** yes.
- **Our take:** a per-family value check in the build script, so every block has its own
  silhouette in greyscale. Our modded stones and metals are where this matters most.

### Stone, ores and metals

**5. Ores told apart by shape, not only colour**
- **Does:** Each ore has its own deposit shape:
  - coal: dark chunks;
  - iron: rounded nuggets;
  - gold: jagged cracks;
  - redstone: dots and glyph-like marks;
  - diamond and emerald: faceted crystals;
  - lapis: flat flakes.
  The ores still read with colour removed.
- **Praised by:** gamepressure ("unique shapes for individual ores"); the colourblind design
  aim (Modrinth, CurseForge).
- **Shot:** Creative tabs (bottom row of Building Blocks), Thumbnail (logo letters).
- **Feasible:** yes, textures only. Our `_s` specular maps can also give each ore type its own
  glint, so shape, colour and shine each tell the ores apart.
- **Our take:** a high-value element for us. The pack has dozens of modded ores, so a shape
  language per family (gems faceted, metals rounded, fuels chunky, dusts dotted) helps
  colourblind friends and everyone else. Draw it over Faithful 32x stone.

**6. Stone, cobble and bricks with clear masonry**
- **Does:** Stone, cobble and the bricks have bold, clean joints with simple 2–3-tone shading.
  Mossy stone bricks show moss as soft patches, not speckle.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery.
- **Shot:** Library (stone bricks, moss), Bastion (blackstone bricks).
- **Feasible:** yes.
- **Our take:** keep Faithful's stone detail, which the owner likes, and borrow only the bolder
  joint lines on bricks and stone bricks.

**7. Metal and gem blocks as chunky bevelled tiles**
- **Does:** Gold, iron and diamond blocks are framed tiles with a bright bevel and a dark rim,
  closer to an ingot stack than a flat colour.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery.
- **Shot:** Bastion (gold blocks).
- **Feasible:** yes. Metal F0 (the 230/231 codes) is already in QF v0.
- **Our take:** the owner already liked Faithful's gold, iron and diamond. Show this as a
  "bevelled" option against Faithful's.

**8. Trypophobia-safe surfaces (end stone, sponge)**
- **Does:** End stone and sponge lose their clustered holes in favour of other patterns.
- **Praised by:** gamepressure (end stone); anvilpacks (sponge).
- **Shot:** The End (end stone).
- **Feasible:** yes.
- **Our take:** cheap to do on our own end stone, sponge and any modded "holey" blocks.

### Wood, building and decorative blocks

**9. Wood with plank boards and character**
- **Does:**
  - Planks run as clear vertical or horizontal boards, with dark seams and visible nails or knots.
  - Logs have deep bark furrows.
  - Doors, trapdoors and fence gates have panel detail.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery.
- **Shot:** Library, Snowy village, Redstone yard (floor).
- **Feasible:** yes (textures). The braced fences in Farm animals are a model change, so not that
  (element 25).
- **Our take:** Faithful-detailed planks with stronger board seams. The test is whether builds
  read better at a distance.

**10. Bookshelves with varied books**
- **Does:** Bookshelves are full-height shelves of mixed colours, sizes and leaning books. Big
  libraries do not look tiled.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery.
- **Shot:** Library.
- **Feasible:** yes. Angelica's CTM `random` or `repeat` can add variants, and it already ships a
  bookshelf CTM rule.
- **Our take:** 4–6 random variants of our own bookshelf, plus the modded bookshelves (BiblioCraft
  and similar).

**11. Utility blocks that show their job**
- **Does:**
  - Crafting table: a cloth over the top and tools on the sides.
  - Furnace: a big glowing mouth.
  - Jukebox: a speaker detail.
  - Chest: dark-banded and bold-latched.
  - Each block's use is clear at a glance.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks (crafting table "3D-looking texture", jukebox speaker, crafting table
  and furnace upgrades).
- **Shot:** Mining (chest, crafting table, furnace).
- **Feasible:**
  - Yes for textures. The chest is an entity texture, so its shape stays.
  - The furnace glow can come from our shader (emission in `_s` alpha).
- **Our take:** a strong fit. Quest Forge's machines (modded furnaces, GT-style machines) all
  benefit from the "show its job" rule. Our own jukebox already has a model.

**12. Glowing blocks that look like light sources**
- **Does:** Glowstone and the redstone lamp are big bright cells with dark grout. Torches have a
  clear flame. They read as lights even in daylight.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery.
- **Shot:** Redstone yard (glowstone wall, lamps).
- **Feasible:** yes. Emission comes from our shader, not OptiFine emissive textures.
- **Our take:** QF v0 uses Faithful glowstone, which the owner liked. The idea to borrow is cell
  shapes for lamps and modded lights, with bright pixels marked emissive.

### Grass, leaves, snow and the overworld

**13. Leaves: dense, dark canopy clusters**
- **Does:** Leaves are dense, darker clusters with fewer see-through holes. Canopies read as
  masses of foliage.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery.
- **Shot:** Dark forest, Farm animals (spruce behind).
- **Feasible:** yes (textures). Our shader already waves leaves and does subsurface light.
- **Our take:** careful. Denser leaves cut the light shafts the owner loves. Test it in
  forest_lit.

**14. Leaves that turn brown when cut off from logs**
- **Does:** leaves left after a tree is felled turn brown. Reported by a user, not seen.
- **Praised by:** resourcepack.net comment ("The leaves turn brown when they're disconnected from
  wood").
- **Shot:** none.
- **Feasible:** **no**, not as described. 1.7.10 leaves only carry "player-placed" and
  "check-decay" bits, not distance to a log.
  - CTM by metadata could tint player-placed leaves differently.
  - The real effect needs code.
- **Our take:** probably skip. If the owner loves it, a small qf-tweaks hook could set a
  metadata bit on orphaned leaves.

### Redstone

**15. Redstone that shows its state**
- **Does:**
  - Repeaters show their delay (1–4) on the texture.
  - Redstone dust and the floor around it show signal-strength numbers.
  - Dispensers, droppers, pistons and comparators have clearer active and inactive faces.
  - TNT has a bold label.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks ("Repeater textures indicate the delay setting"; clearer states);
  holy.gg and anvilpacks via search.
- **Shot:** Redstone yard.
- **Feasible:** partly.
  - Repeater delay is in the block metadata, so CTM `fixed` with `metadata=` could swap the top
    per delay. Untested: CTM may not reach the repeater's own renderer.
  - Dust signal numbers: dust is a special renderer, likely not reachable by CTM, so code is
    needed.
  - On and off faces: yes for blocks that already have on and off icons (lamp, torch, repeater,
    piston).
- **Our take:** worth a trial for repeaters. For dust, a WAILA-style readout (Waila is already
  common on 1.7.10) is cheaper than textures.

### Items and tools

**16. A distinct bottle per potion**
- **Does:** Each potion has its own bottle shape and label, so it can be told apart without its
  colour.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks ("identifiable by shape and label"); CurseForge's own image title
  "CIT + Brewing".
- **Shot:** Brewing, plus [CurseForge "CIT + Brewing"](https://media.forgecdn.net/attachments/614/565/brewing.png).
- **Feasible:** yes, with CIT switched on. 1.7.10 potions are told apart by their damage value,
  which MCPatcher CIT matches.
- **Our take:** high value. The pack's modded potions and the potion-heavy combat need it. We
  draw our own bottle silhouettes per effect family.

**17. A cover per enchanted book**
- **Does:** Enchanted books get a cover per enchantment: a flame for Fire Aspect, a pickaxe for
  Efficiency, and so on. Plain books look different from enchanted ones.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks.
- **Shot:** Brewing (books row), Trading.
- **Feasible:** yes, with CIT (`enchantmentIDs`).
- **Our take:** a strong fit. We have 81 custom enchantments, so covers by category with an icon
  per enchantment would make our enchanting readable. It is a big drawing job; start with the
  categories.

**18. Tools that wear down with durability**
- **Does:** tool textures show chips and cracks as durability falls. Reported by a user.
- **Praised by:** resourcepack.net comment ("I love how the tools visually decay as you use up
  their durability").
- **Shot:** none clear in the gallery.
- **Feasible:** yes, with CIT `damage=` ranges.
- **Our take:** nice for vanilla tools. Modded tools (Tinkers, for example) do their own rendering
  and would not follow.

**19. Food and item icons with charm, some animated**
- **Does:**
  - Food icons are characterful.
  - Rotten flesh has animated flies.
  - The fish are "adorable".
  - Mob buckets show their mob.
- **Praised by:** resourcepack.net comment ("the rotten flesh icon having animated flies", "The
  icons for the fish are adorable"). Sportskeeda's snippet mentions animations with "Easter Eggs,
  inside jokes, and references".
- **Shot:** Creative tabs ("Foodstuffs", "Miscellaneous").
- **Feasible:** yes. Animated item textures work in 1.7.10 through `.mcmeta`.
- **Our take:** small animated touches on a few items: rotten flesh, magic items, quest items.

### GUI, fonts and the menu

**20. Container GUIs with painted scenes**
- **Does:**
  - Crafting, brewing, furnace, trading and enchanting screens are dark-wood frames.
  - Each has a titled tab ("Crafting", "Inventory") and a small painted scene behind the slots: a
    workbench with tools, or a potion lab.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks ("Custom backgrounds and layouts" for the inventory, crafting,
  smelting, enchanting and brewing screens).
- **Shot:** Crafting, Brewing, Trading.
- **Feasible:** partly. We can repaint inside the vanilla GUI frame (`textures/gui/container/*.png`),
  but not move slots or change the layout. Modded GUIs need their own textures, as Faithless's
  separate "Faithless: UI's" pack does for mods.
- **Our take:** fits the "AAA quests" vision. Our own GUIs (quest book, qf-content screens) can go
  furthest; vanilla containers get a frame style.

**21. A brewing chart inside the brewing stand**
- **Does:** The brewing stand screen has a full chart of ingredients and results beside it.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks ("The brewing chart alone has probably saved millions of collective
  alt-tabs to the Minecraft wiki"); holy.gg via search.
- **Shot:** Brewing.
- **Feasible:** **not as a texture.** The chart sits outside the vanilla GUI area, so it needs code:
  a GUI overlay in qf-content or a NEI/quest-book page.
- **Our take:** do it as a page in the quest book or NEI, covering our modded potions too.

**22. Hunger and effects in the food tooltip; boss bars; creative tabs**
- **Does:**
  - Food tooltips show the hunger restored and the effects.
  - The boss bar is custom, with the mob's name plate and an ornate bar.
  - Creative tabs are hanging red banners.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks (food overlay, boss bars).
- **Shot:** Crafting (tooltip), The End (boss bar), Creative tabs.
- **Feasible:**
  - Food tooltip: **no** as a texture; it needs code. AppleCore/AppleSkin-style 1.7.10 mods exist.
  - Boss bar: yes, it lives in `icons.png`.
  - Creative tabs: yes, `tabs.png`, with the shape fixed.
- **Our take:** boss bar styling suits our dungeon bosses. Tooltip info is a mod or qf-content
  job.

**23. A custom, readable font**
- **Does:** a rounder, wider pixel font, which the author presents as easier for dyslexic
  players.
- **Praised by:** gamepressure ("a special font to the game, which makes it easier to read for
  people with dyslexia").
- **Shot:** Main Menu, Crafting.
- **Feasible:** yes. `font/ascii.png` can be replaced, and MCPatcher HDFont allows a finer font.
- **Our take:** our own font, drawn for legibility. Test it with quest text, which is long.

**24. A custom title logo and menu**
- **Does:** The MINECRAFT logo's letters are made of blocks: a waterfall, a portal, a creeper face
  and ore.
- **Praised by:** no specific source; this is from the gallery.
- **Shot:** Main Menu, Thumbnail.
- **Feasible:** yes (`title/minecraft.png`, panorama). Our branding and loading sequence already
  sit there.
- **Our take:** a Quest Forge logo built the same way, in our own art.

### Mobs, sounds and the dimensions

**25. Mobs redesigned in an early-Minecraft style**
- **Does:**
  - Mobs are redrawn in an early-Minecraft style: villagers more human, spiders less spidery
    (for arachnophobia), a more menacing Ender Dragon.
  - Some animals come in breed variants, such as chickens.
- **Praised by:** anvilpacks; the arachnophobia aim (Modrinth). A user reports "cows and pigs
  are broken" on one version (resourcepack.net).
- **Shot:** Farm animals, Thumbnail, The End (dragon).
- **Feasible:**
  - Skins: yes.
  - Variants: yes, with `random_mobs` switched on.
  - Shapes: **no**, there is no CEM.
- **Our take:** random variants for passive mobs are cheap and add life. An arachnophobia option
  as a separate small pack would be kind. Skins otherwise stay vanilla-faithful.

**26. 3D models, swinging lanterns, custom sounds**
- **Does:**
  - Model changes: braced fences, 3D hanging lanterns and chains that swing, a 3D crafting table.
  - Custom sounds: footsteps, ambience and some interactions.
- **Praised by:**
  - Swinging lanterns: resourcepack.net comment ("lanterns and chains swing when hung").
  - The anvilpacks review notes the sounds and calls them "love-it-or-hate-it".
- **Shot:** Snowy village (lantern), Farm animals (fences).
- **Feasible:**
  - Models: **no** with a resource pack. 1.7.10 has no JSON models, no lanterns and no chains.
    Swinging needs animated models, which do not exist here.
  - Sounds: yes (`sounds.json`).
- **Our take:** shape changes only where we own the block (qf-content). Sounds are a separate,
  later question.

### Nether and End (same rules, fewer 1.7.10 blocks)

- **Does:** In the nether, netherrack, blackstone and basalt have strong strata, lava reads as
  bright and flat, and the warped forest has glowing particles. In the end, obsidian is shot
  through with purple and the end portal is drawn.
- **Shot:** Bastion, Warped Deltas, The End.
- **Feasible:** 1.7.10 only has netherrack, soul sand, nether brick, quartz ore, glowstone,
  obsidian and end stone. Those can be retextured. The rest is a reference for our modded
  dimensions (Twilight Forest and about 30 more).
- **Not counted as its own element:** it applies elements 1, 3 and 6.

### Accessibility as a design rule (covers 5, 8, 15, 16, 17, 23, 25)

- **Does:** Faithless's core rule: every block and item is identifiable by shape and pattern
  alone, so the pack works in full colourblindness. The author tested it with colourblind
  players.
- **Praised by:**
  - Sportskeeda: "one of the most accessible resource packs created".
  - anvilpacks: "built this pack *with* disabled players, not just *for* them".
  - gamepressure: "a very positive response from the community".
- **Our take:** adopt the rule, not the art. A greyscale check in `build_qf_textures.py` could
  flag pairs of blocks or items that look the same in grey. This fits the vision of friends on
  weaker PCs, and anyone colourblind among them.

## Common criticisms

- **The style is polarizing.** "The art style will be polarizing" (anvilpacks), and to some it is
  "too busy or too far from the original aesthetic". The owner wants Minecraft's own designs, so
  that is the line to hold.
- **It needs OptiFine.** "you really want OptiFine installed to get the full experience"
  (anvilpacks). Without OptiFine, the CIT potions, the food overlay and the brewing chart do not
  render. On our stack the CIT parts depend on an alpha MCPatcher module.
- **Breakage on updates.** Users report broken pieces:
  - potions missing or invisible;
  - chest, skeleton, iron golem, cows and pigs broken;
  - the GUI invisible, or clashing with Dynamic Skies;
  - crashes on 1.21.5 and breakage on 1.21.11;
  - falling leaves drawn as "giant thin blocks";
  - pale oak missing.
  (resourcepack.net comments.) A pack that overrides this much needs testing with every update,
  and we have about 200 mods.
- **3D models clash with shaders.** The review warns that the 3D models "may cause visual quirks"
  under BSL and Complementary. That fits our own G-buffer lessons.
- **Custom sounds divide people:** "love-it-or-hate-it" (anvilpacks). They can be removed by
  deleting the `sounds` folder.
- **Some features are paywalled or split out.** The Bedrock version is a paid Marketplace pack,
  and mod GUIs are a separate pack. That is not a criticism of the art, but our Faithful licence
  forbids paywalls, so none of that model applies to us.

## Infeasible on our stack, in one list

- 3D block models: fences, lanterns, the crafting table.
- Swinging or animated models.
- Entity model (CEM) changes.
- Colour-coded subtitles.
- The brewing chart beside the GUI, and food tooltips, as textures (they need code).
- Leaves turning brown by distance from a log (needs code).
- Redstone dust signal numbers (probably needs code).
- Anything for blocks 1.7.10 lacks.
- OptiFine emissive textures: replaced by our shader's emission, so the effect is still possible.

Needing a config switch and a trial: CIT (potions, books, tool wear), random mobs and HDFont.
